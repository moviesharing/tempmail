An alternate email address can limit how often a personal inbox is used for low-stakes sign-ups. That is a form of data minimization: sharing a different address with one service. It is not the same as being anonymous.

## What changes

When a service receives a disposable address, it does not automatically receive the address you use for family, work, or recovery. This can make it easier to keep promotional mail and one-off confirmations separate.

The separation only lasts while the address remains available to you. The temporary-mail provider still handles messages sent to it, and the service you signed up for may collect other information about your visit.

## What does not change

An email address is only one identifier. A site may also process account details, payment information, device or browser data, and network information. A temporary inbox does not remove those records, encrypt traffic, or change the terms of a service you use.

The provider that operates the mailbox can process the message and technical details required by its API. In TempMail's case, the provider's published API asks the client to provide a visitor IP address and user-agent. That is one reason the site does not describe temporary email as anonymous.

## Use the right boundary

For a newsletter you are comfortable leaving behind, a disposable address may be convenient. For an account you expect to keep, choose a mailbox you control and can recover. A password manager and unique passwords are better tools for account security; a temporary inbox is not a replacement.

Privacy choices work best when their limits are visible. Check who operates a mailbox, how long messages last, and whether you will need future access before sharing an address.

## A short checklist

- Use temporary mail only for low-risk activity.
- Do not receive sensitive documents or recovery links there.
- Review the provider's own privacy and retention information.
- Avoid entering personal details you do not need to provide.
