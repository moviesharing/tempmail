A temporary email address is a short-lived inbox you can use when a service asks for an email but you do not want that message mixed into your everyday mailbox. It is a useful boundary for low-stakes situations—not a way to make email private.

## What a disposable address is

The address is a real destination, managed by an email provider. Messages sent to it are received and stored by that provider, then made available through an inbox interface. With TempMail, Guerrilla Mail provides the address and mailbox operations.

That means the provider, not the page in your browser, determines whether an address exists, what domain it uses, and how long mail remains available.

## A practical way to use one

Use a temporary address for a one-time download, a low-risk trial, or a test message that you do not need to keep. Copy the address from the page, use it where appropriate, and return to the inbox to read the incoming verification or receipt message.

Before using any disposable inbox, consider whether you may need to reset a password or prove account ownership later. If the answer is yes, use an address you control long term instead.

## What it cannot promise

A temporary address does not encrypt a message or hide the network requests used to create it. The provider may see the address, sender, content, and technical data needed to operate the service. Addresses can expire and may eventually be reused or made accessible to someone else.

Do not use a shared or temporary address for banking, healthcare, identity services, sensitive conversations, or accounts that matter to you.
